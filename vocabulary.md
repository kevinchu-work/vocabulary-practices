## 1. Business & Commerce

**accrue** *(verb)* — To accumulate or build up over time. *"Interest will accrue on the unpaid balance."*

**acquisition** *(noun)* — The purchase of one company by another. *"The acquisition of the smaller firm took six months to complete."*

**affiliate** *(noun/verb)* — A company associated with another; to connect officially. *"The London office is an affiliate of the New York headquarters."*

**allocate** *(verb)* — To distribute resources for a specific purpose. *"The budget was allocated across three departments."*

**appraisal** *(noun)* — A formal assessment of performance or value. *"Her annual appraisal highlighted several areas for improvement."*

**arbitrage** *(noun)* — Taking advantage of price differences between markets. *"Currency arbitrage is common among institutional investors."*

**audit** *(noun/verb)* — An official examination of financial records. *"The company underwent an external audit last quarter."*

**benchmark** *(noun/verb)* — A standard used to compare performance. *"Sales figures are benchmarked against industry averages."*

**bid** *(noun/verb)* — An offer to purchase or supply at a stated price. *"Three companies submitted a bid for the government contract."*

**brand equity** *(noun)* — The value of a brand based on consumer perception. *"Years of quality products have built strong brand equity."*

**breach** *(noun/verb)* — A violation of a rule, law, or contract. *"The supplier was in breach of the delivery agreement."*

**brief** *(noun/verb)* — A concise summary of instructions. *"The marketing brief outlined the campaign objectives."*

**broker** *(noun/verb)* — A person acting as an intermediary in transactions. *"We used a freight broker to handle the shipping."*

**bulk** *(adjective/noun)* — In large quantities. *"Buying in bulk reduces the cost per unit significantly."*

**capacity** *(noun)* — The maximum amount something can produce or hold. *"The factory is operating at full capacity."*

**cartel** *(noun)* — A group of firms that agree to fix prices. *"The oil cartel agreed to cut production."*

**cash flow** *(noun)* — The movement of money into and out of a business. *"Poor cash flow is one of the main reasons businesses fail."*

**clause** *(noun)* — A specific section within a contract. *"The penalty clause states that late delivery incurs a fine."*

**clientele** *(noun)* — The regular customers of a business. *"The restaurant built a loyal clientele over ten years."*

**collaborate** *(verb)* — To work jointly with others. *"The two firms collaborated on a joint research project."*

**commodity** *(noun)* — A raw material that can be bought and sold. *"Oil is one of the world's most traded commodities."*

**comply** *(verb)* — To act in accordance with rules or requests. *"All suppliers must comply with the new safety standards."*

**concession** *(noun)* — A reduction in price or terms granted in negotiation. *"The retailer made a concession on the price to close the deal."*

**conglomerate** *(noun)* — A large corporation owning businesses in different industries. *"The conglomerate has interests in media, retail, and finance."*

**consortium** *(noun)* — A group of organisations working together. *"A consortium of banks funded the infrastructure project."*

**contingency** *(noun)* — A provision for a possible future event. *"The project budget includes a 10% contingency."*

**counterfeit** *(adjective/noun)* — Fake or imitation, intended to deceive. *"Counterfeit goods cost legitimate businesses billions each year."*

**creditor** *(noun)* — A person or company owed money. *"The creditors agreed to a restructuring plan."*

**criteria** *(noun, plural)* — Standards by which something is judged. *"The selection criteria include experience and qualifications."*

**curtail** *(verb)* — To reduce or limit. *"Rising costs forced the company to curtail its expansion plans."*

**deadlock** *(noun)* — A situation where no progress is possible. *"Negotiations reached a deadlock over pay."*

**debtor** *(noun)* — A person or company that owes money. *"The bank pursued the debtor through the courts."*

**deficit** *(noun)* — The amount by which something falls short. *"The trade deficit widened to £5 billion."*

**delegate** *(verb/noun)* — To assign work to someone else; a representative. *"She delegated the task to her assistant."*

**demographic** *(noun/adjective)* — Relating to population statistics. *"The campaign targeted a younger demographic."*

**depreciate** *(verb)* — To decrease in value over time. *"Vehicles depreciate quickly in the first year."*

**differentiate** *(verb)* — To make a product or service distinct. *"The brand differentiates itself through superior customer service."*

**directive** *(noun)* — An official instruction or order. *"The EU issued a directive on data protection."*

**distribution** *(noun)* — The process of supplying goods to retailers. *"The company has a strong distribution network across Europe."*

**diversify** *(verb)* — To expand into different areas or products. *"The retailer diversified into online sales."*

**dividend** *(noun)* — A sum paid to shareholders from company profits. *"Shareholders received a dividend of 20p per share."*

**downsize** *(verb)* — To reduce the size of a workforce. *"The firm downsized following poor quarterly results."*

**due diligence** *(noun)* — Research done before a business transaction. *"Investors conducted thorough due diligence before acquiring the company."*

**embargo** *(noun)* — An official ban on trade with a country. *"The trade embargo severely affected the country's exports."*

**endorse** *(verb)* — To publicly support or approve something. *"The celebrity endorsed the brand's new product line."*

**entrepreneur** *(noun)* — A person who starts and runs a business. *"She became a successful entrepreneur after leaving her corporate job."*

**equity** *(noun)* — The value of shares issued by a company; ownership stake. *"The startup raised £2 million in equity funding."*

**escalate** *(verb)* — To increase in intensity or seriousness. *"The dispute escalated into a formal grievance."*

**ethics** *(noun)* — Moral principles governing behaviour. *"The board is reviewing its corporate ethics policy."*

**expenditure** *(noun)* — The amount of money spent. *"Capital expenditure rose by 12% this year."*

**facilitate** *(verb)* — To make a process easier. *"The new software facilitates faster data processing."*

**feasibility** *(noun)* — The possibility of doing something successfully. *"A feasibility study was conducted before the project began."*

**fluctuate** *(verb)* — To rise and fall irregularly. *"Demand tends to fluctuate with the seasons."*

**forecast** *(noun/verb)* — A prediction of future trends. *"The sales forecast for Q3 looks optimistic."*

**franchise** *(noun/verb)* — A licence to operate a business model. *"They opened a franchise of the popular coffee chain."*

**gross** *(adjective)* — Total before deductions. *"The company's gross revenue was £10 million last year."*

**incentive** *(noun)* — Something that motivates a particular action. *"Staff bonuses serve as an incentive to meet targets."*

**inception** *(noun)* — The beginning or establishment of something. *"Sales have grown steadily since the company's inception."*

**incorporate** *(verb)* — To form a company legally; to include. *"The firm was incorporated in England and Wales in 2005."*

**insolvency** *(noun)* — The inability to pay debts. *"The business filed for insolvency after mounting debts."*

**interim** *(adjective/noun)* — Temporary; in the meantime. *"An interim manager was appointed while the CEO was on leave."*

**invoice** *(noun/verb)* — A document requesting payment. *"Please send the invoice within 30 days of delivery."*

**joint venture** *(noun)* — A business collaboration between two parties. *"The two firms formed a joint venture to enter the Chinese market."*

**leverage** *(noun/verb)* — Using borrowed capital; to use something to maximum advantage. *"The company used leverage to finance the acquisition."*

**liability** *(noun)* — A legal obligation; a debt. *"The company's total liabilities exceeded its assets."*

**liquidate** *(verb)* — To wind up a business; to sell assets for cash. *"The firm was liquidated after failing to attract investors."*

**logistics** *(noun)* — The organisation of complex operations. *"The logistics team manages delivery across 15 countries."*

**lucrative** *(adjective)* — Producing a large profit. *"The contract proved to be highly lucrative."*

**mandate** *(noun/verb)* — An official authorisation; to require by authority. *"The board gave the CEO a mandate to restructure the company."*

**margin** *(noun)* — The difference between cost and selling price. *"Profit margins have been squeezed by rising raw material costs."*

**merger** *(noun)* — The combination of two companies into one. *"The merger created the largest bank in the UK."*

**milestone** *(noun)* — A significant achievement or event. *"Reaching one million customers was a major milestone."*

**monopoly** *(noun)* — Exclusive control of a market. *"The regulator investigated the company for monopoly behaviour."*

**niche** *(noun/adjective)* — A specialised segment of the market. *"They found a niche in eco-friendly packaging."*

**obsolete** *(adjective)* — No longer in use or needed. *"The old system became obsolete after the software upgrade."*

**outsource** *(verb)* — To hire external companies to perform services. *"The company outsourced its IT support to reduce costs."*

**overhead** *(noun)* — Regular operating costs not directly linked to production. *"High overheads are eroding profit margins."*

**penetrate** *(verb)* — To enter a new market successfully. *"The brand struggled to penetrate the highly competitive US market."*

**portfolio** *(noun)* — A range of products, services, or investments held by one entity. *"The company's portfolio includes luxury and budget brands."*

**premium** *(noun/adjective)* — A higher price; of superior quality. *"Premium products command a higher price point."*

**procurement** *(noun)* — The process of obtaining goods or services. *"The procurement team handles all supplier contracts."*

**proposal** *(noun)* — A formal plan put forward for consideration. *"The agency submitted a detailed proposal for the campaign."*

**prototype** *(noun)* — A first version of a product used for testing. *"The prototype was tested with a focus group before launch."*

**provision** *(noun)* — The action of providing something; a sum set aside. *"The accounts include a provision for bad debts."*

**quota** *(noun)* — A limited amount assigned to someone. *"Each salesperson has a monthly sales quota to meet."*

**rebrand** *(verb)* — To change the image of a company or product. *"The company rebranded after a major PR crisis."*

**reciprocal** *(adjective)* — Given or felt by each towards the other. *"They agreed on a reciprocal trade arrangement."*

**remunerate** *(verb)* — To pay someone for work. *"Senior staff are well remunerated for their expertise."*

**retain** *(verb)* — To keep; to continue to have. *"The company works hard to retain its top talent."*

**revenue** *(noun)* — Income generated by a business. *"Annual revenue grew by 18% compared to last year."*

**rival** *(noun/verb)* — A competitor; to compete with. *"The two rivals are competing for the same government contract."*

**scrutinise** *(verb)* — To examine closely and critically. *"Auditors scrutinised every line of the accounts."*

**solvent** *(adjective)* — Able to pay debts; financially stable. *"The company remained solvent despite the economic downturn."*

**stakeholder** *(noun)* — A person with an interest in the success of a project or company. *"All stakeholders were invited to the consultation."*

**subsidiary** *(noun)* — A company controlled by another. *"The UK subsidiary reports to the parent company in Germany."*

**surplus** *(noun)* — An amount left over after requirements are met. *"The firm recorded a budget surplus of £400,000."*

**synergy** *(noun)* — The combined benefit of two things working together. *"The merger created synergies that reduced operating costs."*

**tariff** *(noun)* — A tax on imports or exports. *"New tariffs on steel imports affected manufacturers."*

**tender** *(noun/verb)* — A formal bid for a contract; to submit a bid. *"Three companies tendered for the construction contract."*

**trademark** *(noun)* — A symbol or word legally registered to represent a company. *"The logo is a registered trademark."*

**turnover** *(noun)* — The total sales of a business; also the rate of staff replacement. *"Annual turnover reached £50 million for the first time."*

**viable** *(adjective)* — Capable of working or being successful. *"The board assessed whether the new product line was commercially viable."*

**volatile** *(adjective)* — Liable to change rapidly and unpredictably. *"Currency markets have been particularly volatile this year."*

**warranty** *(noun)* — A written guarantee to repair or replace a product. *"The appliance comes with a two-year warranty."*

**wholesaler** *(noun)* — A company selling goods in large quantities to retailers. *"They buy stock directly from a wholesaler to keep costs down."*

## 2. Finance & Economics

**amortise** *(verb)* — To gradually pay off a debt over time. *"The loan was amortised over 20 years."*

**annuity** *(noun)* — A fixed sum paid regularly, typically annually. *"She receives an annuity from her pension fund."*

**appreciation** *(noun)* — An increase in the value of an asset. *"The appreciation of property prices has outpaced wage growth."*

**austerity** *(noun)* — Strict economic policies to reduce government spending. *"The government's austerity measures cut public services."*

**bail out** *(verb)* — To rescue a company or individual from financial difficulty. *"The government bailed out the struggling bank."*

**balance sheet** *(noun)* — A financial statement showing assets and liabilities. *"The balance sheet revealed a healthy surplus."*

**bear market** *(noun)* — A period when share prices are falling. *"Investors were cautious during the prolonged bear market."*

**bond** *(noun)* — A fixed-income investment issued by a government or company. *"The government issued bonds to raise capital."*

**bull market** *(noun)* — A period when share prices are rising. *"The bull market of the 1990s created enormous wealth."*

**collateral** *(noun)* — Property or assets pledged as security for a loan. *"The house was used as collateral for the business loan."*

**compound interest** *(noun)* — Interest calculated on both the principal and accumulated interest. *"Compound interest can significantly increase long-term savings."*

**contraction** *(noun)* — A period of economic decline. *"The economy entered contraction following the financial crisis."*

**credit rating** *(noun)* — An assessment of a borrower's creditworthiness. *"A poor credit rating made it difficult to obtain loans."*

**deflation** *(noun)* — A general decrease in prices over time. *"Deflation can harm economic growth by reducing consumer spending."*

**deregulate** *(verb)* — To remove government regulations from an industry. *"The financial sector was deregulated in the 1980s."*

**derivative** *(noun)* — A financial contract whose value is based on an underlying asset. *"Derivatives are often used to hedge against risk."*

**devalue** *(verb)* — To reduce the official value of a currency. *"The government was forced to devalue the pound."*

**disposable income** *(noun)* — Income remaining after taxes and essential expenses. *"Rising inflation is reducing households' disposable income."*

**diversification** *(noun)* — Spreading investments to reduce risk. *"Diversification is a key principle of sound investing."*

**fiscal** *(adjective)* — Relating to government finances or taxation. *"The Chancellor announced new fiscal measures in the Budget."*

**flotation** *(noun)* — The launch of a company on a stock exchange. *"The company's flotation on the London Stock Exchange raised £200m."*

**foreign exchange** *(noun)* — The exchange of one currency for another. *"Foreign exchange markets operate 24 hours a day."*

**GDP (Gross Domestic Product)** *(noun)* — The total value of goods and services produced by a country. *"UK GDP grew by 2.1% last year."*

**gilt** *(noun)* — A UK government bond, considered low-risk. *"Gilts are considered a safe investment during uncertain times."*

**hedge** *(verb/noun)* — To reduce financial risk; an investment used to offset risk. *"The fund hedged its exposure to currency fluctuations."*

**hyperinflation** *(noun)* — Extremely rapid and uncontrolled price increases. *"Hyperinflation in the 1920s wiped out savings overnight."*

**inflation** *(noun)* — A general increase in prices over time. *"High inflation erodes the purchasing power of money."*

**IPO (Initial Public Offering)** *(noun)* — The first sale of a company's shares to the public. *"The tech company's IPO valued it at £1 billion."*

**liquidity** *(noun)* — The availability of cash or easily convertible assets. *"The bank maintained sufficient liquidity to meet customer withdrawals."*

**macroeconomics** *(noun)* — The study of the economy as a whole. *"Macroeconomics considers factors like unemployment and inflation."*

**microeconomics** *(noun)* — The study of individual businesses and consumer behaviour. *"Microeconomics analyses how price changes affect demand."*

**monetary policy** *(noun)* — Government actions to control money supply and interest rates. *"The central bank uses monetary policy to manage inflation."*

**net worth** *(noun)* — The total value of assets minus liabilities. *"His net worth grew substantially during the property boom."*

**nominal** *(adjective)* — In name only; not adjusted for inflation. *"Nominal wages have risen, but real wages have fallen."*

**offshore** *(adjective/adverb)* — Located abroad, often for tax purposes. *"The company holds assets in an offshore account."*

**private equity** *(noun)* — Investment capital not listed on public exchanges. *"The firm was bought out by a private equity company."*

**quantitative easing** *(noun)* — A monetary policy of creating money to stimulate the economy. *"The Bank of England used quantitative easing during the financial crisis."*

**recession** *(noun)* — A period of economic decline, typically two consecutive quarters of negative growth. *"The country entered a recession after the financial crisis."*

**refinance** *(verb)* — To replace an existing loan with a new one, usually at a better rate. *"They refinanced their mortgage to get a lower interest rate."*

**remittance** *(noun)* — Money sent to another country. *"Remittances are a vital source of income in many developing nations."*

**return on investment (ROI)** *(noun)* — The gain or loss generated relative to the amount invested. *"The project delivered a return on investment of 25%."*

**securities** *(noun, plural)* — Financial instruments such as shares and bonds. *"The fund invests in a range of securities."*

**speculate** *(verb)* — To invest in risky assets in the hope of making a profit. *"Many investors speculated in property during the boom."*

**sterling** *(noun/adjective)* — British currency; of high quality. *"The pound sterling has weakened against the dollar."*

**stimulus** *(noun)* — Economic measures taken to encourage growth. *"The government introduced a stimulus package during the downturn."*

**subsidy** *(noun)* — Financial assistance given by the government. *"Farmers receive a subsidy to maintain food production."*

**takeover** *(noun)* — The acquisition of one company by another. *"The hostile takeover bid was rejected by the board."*

**tax relief** *(noun)* — A reduction in tax liability. *"There is tax relief available on pension contributions."*

**underwrite** *(verb)* — To take financial responsibility for; to guarantee. *"The bank agreed to underwrite the bond issue."*

**valuation** *(noun)* — An estimate of the value of something. *"The company's valuation reached £500 million after the latest funding round."*

**venture capital** *(noun)* — Funding for start-up businesses. *"They secured £1 million in venture capital to launch the product."*

**write off** *(verb)* — To cancel a bad debt; to declare something as a loss. *"The bank wrote off £200 million in bad loans."*

**yield** *(noun/verb)* — The income from an investment; to produce. *"The bond yields 3.5% per annum."*

## 3. Law & Regulation

**acquit** *(verb)* — To formally declare someone not guilty. *"The jury acquitted the defendant after deliberating for two days."*

**adjudicate** *(verb)* — To make an official judgement on a dispute. *"A panel of experts was appointed to adjudicate the matter."*

**affidavit** *(noun)* — A written statement made under oath. *"She submitted an affidavit to the court."*

**allegation** *(noun)* — A claim not yet proven. *"He denied the allegations of misconduct."*

**appeal** *(noun/verb)* — A request to a higher court to reconsider a decision. *"The defendant lodged an appeal against the sentence."*

**arbitration** *(noun)* — A method of resolving disputes outside of court. *"The contract stipulates that disputes be resolved by arbitration."*

**binding** *(adjective)* — Legally obligatory. *"The agreement is legally binding on both parties."*

**bribery** *(noun)* — The act of offering something to influence someone. *"The executive was charged with bribery."*

**claimant** *(noun)* — A person making a legal claim. *"The claimant sought compensation for loss of earnings."*

**compliance** *(noun)* — The act of obeying rules and regulations. *"The company is reviewed annually for regulatory compliance."*

**confidentiality** *(noun)* — The state of keeping information private. *"The agreement includes a strict confidentiality clause."*

**convict** *(verb/noun)* — To find someone guilty; a person found guilty. *"He was convicted of fraud and sentenced to three years."*

**copyright** *(noun)* — Legal right protecting creative work. *"The image is protected by copyright."*

**culpable** *(adjective)* — Deserving blame or censure. *"The report found the manager culpable for the safety failure."*

**damages** *(noun, plural)* — Financial compensation awarded by a court. *"The claimant was awarded £50,000 in damages."*

**defendant** *(noun)* — A person accused in a legal case. *"The defendant pleaded not guilty."*

**disclosure** *(noun)* — The act of making information known. *"Full disclosure of financial interests is required."*

**enforce** *(verb)* — To ensure compliance with a law or rule. *"Regulations are enforced by the relevant authority."*

**entitlement** *(noun)* — A right to receive or do something. *"Employees have a legal entitlement to paid leave."*

**fraud** *(noun)* — Wrongful deception for financial gain. *"He was arrested on charges of fraud."*

**grievance** *(noun)* — A formal complaint. *"She filed a formal grievance with HR."*

**immunity** *(noun)* — Protection from prosecution or penalty. *"The witness was granted immunity in exchange for testimony."*

**indemnify** *(verb)* — To compensate for loss; to protect against liability. *"The supplier agreed to indemnify the client for any losses."*

**infringe** *(verb)* — To actively break a rule or law. *"The product infringed the competitor's patent."*

**injunction** *(noun)* — A court order requiring someone to stop an action. *"The company obtained an injunction to prevent publication."*

**intellectual property** *(noun)* — Creations of the mind protected by law. *"They took legal action to protect their intellectual property."*

**jurisdiction** *(noun)* — The territory over which legal authority extends. *"The case falls outside this court's jurisdiction."*

**liable** *(adjective)* — Legally responsible for something. *"The driver was held liable for the accident."*

**litigation** *(noun)* — The process of taking a case to court. *"They hoped to avoid costly litigation."*

**mediation** *(noun)* — Dispute resolution using a neutral third party. *"Both parties agreed to mediation before going to court."*

**negligence** *(noun)* — Failure to take proper care, resulting in harm. *"The hospital was sued for medical negligence."*

**non-disclosure agreement (NDA)** *(noun)* — A contract preventing the sharing of confidential information. *"All employees were required to sign an NDA."*

**patent** *(noun/verb)* — An exclusive right granted for an invention. *"The company patented its new manufacturing process."*

**penalty** *(noun)* — A punishment for breaking a rule. *"Late payment incurs a penalty of 5% of the invoice value."*

**plaintiff** *(noun)* — The person who brings a case to court. *"The plaintiff claimed damages of £100,000."*

**precedent** *(noun)* — An earlier decision used to guide future cases. *"The ruling set a precedent for similar cases."*

**prosecute** *(verb)* — To take legal proceedings against someone. *"The company was prosecuted for environmental violations."*

**repeal** *(verb)* — To revoke or cancel a law. *"Parliament voted to repeal the outdated legislation."*

**settlement** *(noun)* — An agreement reached to resolve a dispute. *"The case was resolved out of court by a financial settlement."*

**statute** *(noun)* — A written law passed by a government. *"The statute requires all employers to provide safe working conditions."*

**testimony** *(noun)* — Evidence given under oath. *"His testimony was crucial to the outcome of the trial."*

**tribunal** *(noun)* — A specialist judicial body. *"The employment tribunal ruled in favour of the claimant."*

**verdict** *(noun)* — The decision reached by a court. *"The jury returned a unanimous verdict of guilty."*

**waiver** *(noun)* — The voluntary abandonment of a legal right. *"She signed a waiver before participating in the activity."*

**whistleblower** *(noun)* — A person who reports illegal or unethical activity. *"The whistleblower exposed the company's financial irregularities."*

## 4. Politics & Government

**accountability** *(noun)* — The fact of being responsible and answerable for one's actions. *"Ministers must be held to accountability for their decisions."*

**amendment** *(noun)* — A change made to a law or official document. *"An amendment to the bill was passed by Parliament."*

**autocracy** *(noun)* — A system of government by one person with absolute power. *"The country moved towards autocracy after the coup."*

**ballot** *(noun/verb)* — A process of voting; to vote. *"Voters cast their ballot in the general election."*

**bipartisan** *(adjective)* — Involving agreement between two parties. *"The legislation passed with bipartisan support."*

**bureaucracy** *(noun)* — A system of complex administrative rules and processes. *"Businesses complained about the burden of bureaucracy."*

**censure** *(noun/verb)* — An official expression of disapproval. *"The MP received a formal censure for his conduct."*

**civil service** *(noun)* — The body of permanent government employees. *"She joined the civil service after graduating."*

**coalition** *(noun)* — An alliance of political parties governing together. *"A coalition government was formed after the hung parliament."*

**constituency** *(noun)* — An area whose voters elect a parliamentary representative. *"He has represented the same constituency for 15 years."*

**coup** *(noun)* — A sudden, illegal seizure of power from a government. *"The military staged a coup and dissolved parliament."*

**diplomacy** *(noun)* — The management of international relations. *"Diplomacy is often preferable to military intervention."*

**dissolution** *(noun)* — The formal ending of a parliament or assembly. *"The Prime Minister called for the dissolution of Parliament."*

**empower** *(verb)* — To give authority or legal power to. *"The legislation empowered local councils to set their own rates."*

**enact** *(verb)* — To make a bill into law. *"Parliament enacted new legislation on data protection."*

**faction** *(noun)* — A group within a larger organisation with different aims. *"The party was divided by rival factions."*

**geopolitics** *(noun)* — The influence of geography on international politics. *"Energy security has become a key issue in geopolitics."*

**governance** *(noun)* — The way in which an organisation or country is run. *"Good governance requires transparency and accountability."*

**incumbent** *(noun/adjective)* — The current holder of a political office. *"The incumbent Prime Minister faces a tough election."*

**legislature** *(noun)* — The law-making body of a state. *"The legislature debated the bill for three days."*

**lobby** *(verb/noun)* — To seek to influence politicians; a group that does this. *"The pharmaceutical industry lobbied hard against the new regulations."*

**manifesto** *(noun)* — A public declaration of a party's policies and intentions. *"The party launched its election manifesto yesterday."*

**nationalise** *(verb)* — To transfer private industry to state ownership. *"The railway was nationalised in the 1940s."*

**opposition** *(noun)* — The main political party not in government. *"The opposition challenged the government over the new policy."*

**partisan** *(adjective)* — Strongly supporting a particular party or cause. *"The debate became increasingly partisan."*

**petition** *(noun/verb)* — A formal request signed by many people; to appeal formally. *"Over 100,000 people signed the petition."*

**populism** *(noun)* — Political ideas that appeal to ordinary people against perceived elites. *"Populism has reshaped politics across Europe."*

**privatise** *(verb)* — To transfer state ownership to the private sector. *"The water industry was privatised in 1989."*

**ratify** *(verb)* — To give formal approval to a treaty or agreement. *"All member states must ratify the treaty."*

**referendum** *(noun)* — A public vote on a single political question. *"The country held a referendum on EU membership."*

**regime** *(noun)* — A system or method of government, often authoritarian. *"International pressure mounted on the authoritarian regime."*

**sanction** *(noun/verb)* — An official penalty; to impose such a penalty. *"Economic sanctions were imposed on the country."*

**scrutiny** *(noun)* — Close examination by an authority or the public. *"The proposal is subject to parliamentary scrutiny."*

**sovereignty** *(noun)* — Full, independent governing authority. *"Brexit was largely driven by concerns over national sovereignty."*

**suppress** *(verb)* — To forcibly put an end to something. *"The government suppressed protests in the capital."*

**veto** *(noun/verb)* — The right to reject a decision; to reject. *"The President used his veto to block the legislation."*

**welfare** *(noun)* — Government financial support for those in need. *"Welfare spending has increased significantly."*

## 5. Workplace & Human Resources

**absenteeism** *(noun)* — Habitual absence from work without good reason. *"High absenteeism is costing the company £2 million annually."*

**accreditation** *(noun)* — Official recognition that a standard has been met. *"The training programme has received professional accreditation."*

**attrition** *(noun)* — The gradual reduction of a workforce through natural departures. *"Natural attrition reduced headcount without the need for redundancies."*

**autonomy** *(noun)* — The right to make decisions independently. *"Employees value having autonomy in how they manage their workload."*

**collective bargaining** *(noun)* — Negotiation between employers and trade unions. *"Collective bargaining resulted in a 4% pay rise."*

**competency** *(noun)* — A skill or quality required for a role. *"Leadership is a core competency for senior managers."*

**conciliation** *(noun)* — Helping two parties in a dispute reach agreement. *"ACAS offered conciliation services to resolve the dispute."*

**contractor** *(noun)* — A person or company hired to do specific work. *"The project was carried out by an external contractor."*

**disciplinary** *(adjective)* — Relating to punishment or corrective action. *"A disciplinary hearing was scheduled for the following week."*
